You know, an issue that our present Pope, Benedict XVI, finds extremely important is the relationship between the Christian religion and modern democracy. As Cardinal Ratzinger, he wrote extensively on this subject over many years, and has even now made it central to his concerns as Pope. Here's the difficulty as Benedict XVI sees it. Especially in the wake of the great totalitarianisms of the 20th century, some theorists of democracy, some political philosophers, hold that any claim to absolute truth is inimical to the exercise of real freedom. They mean that whenever a group or individual claims to know the truth, this will be threatening to the freedom of others. So much so that truth appears to belong to the realm of anti-democratic intolerance. How often, for example, do you hear, in our culture, who are you to impose your views on me? If you read now the political philosophies of John Rawls and Richard Rorty in this country, Jürgen Habermas over in Europe, you'll find this view.
